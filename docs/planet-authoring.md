# ChapterOne: 3D plane authoring, spherical gameplay

The production world is `Assets/Scenes/Galaxy.unity`, backed by `Assets/Resources/Galaxy/ChapterOne/PlanetChapterOne.prefab`. Use **BackHome > ChapterOne > Open World**. The scene retains its original GUID and build entry, so death/restart, ship takeoff and hologram travel all use this world. There is no separate prototype world.

## Editing

Select **PlanetChapterOne > Frame 3D Plane** for perspective editing. **Top View** is optional. Frame isolates the world in Scene View so HUD and background objects do not intercept clicks; **Show Whole Scene** exits isolation. These controls do not change the Game camera. Use Unity's usual orbit, pan, zoom and W/E/R transform tools.

**Paint in Scene View** edits terrain or raises, lowers and sets ground height. Shift reverses raise/lower or resets terrain/height to its default. The inspector also provides a cached cell grid, eyedropper, flood fill, whole-map fill and terrain preset load/save. The cursor hits the visible terrain mesh even in perspective. A stroke is one Undo operation; the preview mesh and picking collider refresh on release. Alt navigates and Esc exits painting.

Under **Authoring Plane**, select a visible model to edit its saved `PlanetPlacement` handle. Use **Place On Plane**, **Snap To Ground**, **Duplicate Selected Prop** and **Remove Prop** in its inspector. Ctrl+D also creates an independent gameplay object: the editor repairs copied bindings while preserving the handle's position, rotation and scale. Area markers renamed or moved between existing stage groups keep matching runtime names and stage membership. Disabling a handle also omits its gameplay object. Removing an inherited prefab child from a scene instance records this disabled override; removing an added object or a child in Prefab Mode deletes its handle and runtime target.

Add rigid environment prefabs through **New prop prefab > Place New Prop On Plane**. **Environment Palette / Scatter** opens the existing Grass, Rocks and Trees palettes with radius, spacing, density, yaw and scale settings. Paint on the plane; Shift erases the chosen category. Each scatter stroke is one Undo operation. The palette is stored on **Authoring Plane/Environment**.

**Terrain Settings** edits the underlying grid and tileset. The continent generator and tileset importer operate on this data too. **Edit Gameplay Settings** on a placement selects its corresponding runtime object when you need to change a gameplay component. Edit positions through the plane handles: their poses are authoritative in Play.

Save the scene for instance overrides, or edit the prefab in Prefab Mode for reusable changes. Press Play to project the world onto the sphere; stopping restores plane editing. Keep **Reload Scene** enabled in Enter Play Mode Settings. Keep the world at unit scale and both **Runtime Planet** and **Authoring Plane** at identity local transforms. Leave **Runtime Planet** inactive while editing.

Both **Authoring Plane/Areas** and **Runtime Planet/Areas** contain **Stage A**, **Stage B**, **Stage C** and **Stage D**. Their `PlanetStage` identities correspond to Grass, GlowingMushroom, Void and Crimson respectively (including both bright and dark tiles). Each stage contains three ordinary areas and three king areas: A1–A3 / KingA1–KingA3 through D1–D3 / KingD1–KingD3. Stage A also contains Camp. The scene's CreatureSpawner has six points per stage, ordered ordinary area then king area for each pair. B–D initially use the same creature prefabs, counts, radii and respawn timers as A; edit individual rows to customize them. Keep stage groups at identity local transforms. Spawn point area IDs can use a unique area name such as `A1` or an explicit path such as `Stage A/A1`; direct Areas children remain supported.

The **CreatureSpawner** Inspector groups **Spawn Points** into foldouts matching the planet's **Areas/Stage A–D** hierarchy, including empty stages. **Add Spawn Point** selects an existing area in that stage; create areas on the planet first. The row menu moves points up/down within a stage or removes them. Grouping follows the area's `PlanetStage`, including inactive stages, and preserves the serialized rows and king navigation order. Unresolved area IDs appear under **Unassigned** for correction.

Move, rotate and resize areas under **Authoring Plane/Areas**. Their linked runtime areas now update in Edit mode, including Undo/Redo and scene/prefab saves, so the saved spherical positions follow the flat map without waiting for Play. The flat handles remain authoritative; synchronization preserves their authored coordinates and keeps Runtime Planet inactive.

`PlanetStageProgression` on Runtime Planet starts with only A accessible. Defeating every configured area king in a stage once completes it and unlocks only the next stage. Each stage requires its three kings, progressing A → B → C → D. Respawned kings do not count twice; stages without configured kings do not complete automatically. Boss requirements come from the scene's CreatureSpawner. In a built game, progress (including partial boss victories) is saved locally in PlayerPrefs under `BackHome.Stages.v1.ChapterOne`, surviving death, scene travel and application restarts. In the Unity Editor, every new Play starts at Stage A with no defeated bosses; progression stays in memory across death/restart and scene travel within that Play. Play mode never reads or overwrites the persistent save, and the session resets even with domain reload disabled.

Locked stage area roots remain inactive and their mobs/kings are not instantiated. Unlocking creates only the newly available dens, preserving existing creatures and respawn timers. Environment streaming caches each prop's stage from its parent `PlanetStage`, or from the terrain at its projected position when it is in the shared Environment hierarchy. A locked stage overrides camera/player proximity; unlocked content still uses the existing show/hide distances. The walker checks the complete movement arc against locked terrain, including large steps. Grass uses A; mushroom, void and crimson use B, C and D. A stage map built once at startup assigns dirt roads, sand/clay and water to the nearest biome by grid distance (longitude wraps), so roads cannot bypass a locked biome. Terrain meshes remain shared, and serialized decoration assets stay in memory while their GameObjects are inactive.

## Runtime and performance

### Locked-stage clouds

`PlanetStageFog` on Runtime Planet covers locked B/C/D land with rounded, pale blue/cream cloud banks. It reads `PlanetStageProgression.StageAtCell`, including the same road/shore ownership as movement. Open ocean stays visible. A saved unlocked stage creates no fog. A stage opening animates its clouds down into the terrain over 0.85 seconds, then releases that stage's meshes, material and objects. Disabling/unloading the component also cancels reveals and releases its resources.

The effect deliberately uses opaque geometry with depth writes instead of stacked transparent particles or volumetric ray marching. One shared material per locked stage, 8×8-cell spatial chunks, baked vertex lighting and shader-only breathing keep the steady-state CPU work at zero callbacks. There are no texture samples, depth/color copies, colliders, shadows, light probes, post-processing or per-cloud GameObjects. Geometry is uploaded once and its readable CPU copy released. Overlapping rounded domes provide the entire coverage, with no rectangular floor. Their vertices follow the curved terrain and their lower rings remain embedded during breathing, preventing exposed flat aprons or floating cut edges. Terrain heights are sampled only when building. Hidden lower hemispheres and redundant pole/seam vertices are omitted. The shader uses `UnityPerMaterial` for SRP Batcher compatibility. Spatial chunks retain frustum culling; batching does not mean the entire planet is a single draw call.

Tune `cloudHeight`, `edgePadding`, `movementClearance` and `revealDuration` on Runtime Planet's component; tune the three colors and breathing on `Resources/Galaxy/Shaders/StageCloud.mat`. The default `edgePadding = 0.7` broadens the rounded domes. A quarter-cell south-west bias follows the terrain splat transition into neighboring cells, with reduced placement jitter to keep border coverage consistent. Main domes follow the cell axes so narrow cells keep their full coverage; decorative crowns retain rotation. The effect runs in Play, leaving plane authoring unobscured. After changing settings during Play, `Rebuild()` recreates the visual resources and movement boundaries using current progression. It does not rebuild gameplay stage ownership after terrain edits.

Movement follows the generated cloud footprints, including their offset and decorative crowns. `PlanetWalker` passes its body radius to `PlanetStageProgression.ConstrainMove`, which sweeps both the existing terrain access rules and the nearby cloud ellipses. `movementClearance = 0.35` leaves a small gap beyond the body. Footprints are generated with the meshes and indexed by source cell, including longitude wrapping; movement queries allocate no managed memory and add no physics colliders or renderers. A player already inside an expanded edge may move outward without moving deeper. A bank keeps its boundary throughout its reveal, then releases it with the visual resources; disabling/rebuilding follows the same lifecycle. Stage ownership, spawning rules and saved progression remain separate from this visual boundary.

The current ChapterOne prefab covers 743 locked land cells with 34 chunks and 47,824 vertices at fresh progress (12% fewer vertices after removing the floor). Unity's Editor reports about 3.54 MiB for those meshes; building the terrain-conforming clouds and footprint index measured about 65–80 ms once at initialization on the development PC. The isolated movement regression measured 3.11 ms total for 1,000 short moves with zero managed allocations. These are Editor measurements, **not** a mobile frame-time benchmark. Fog adds geometry and pixel shading; target-device profiling remains necessary before claiming no FPS impact.

Research informing the design (reviewed 2026-11-01): [Android's materials/shaders guidance](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/materials) recommends simple lighting, limited texture sampling and minimizing layered transparency; [Unity's custom shader optimization guidance](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/SL-ShaderPerformance.html) covers vertex calculations, precision and early depth rejection; [Unity's SRP Batcher shader requirements](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/urp/shaders-in-universalrp-srp-batcher.html) specify the material constant buffer layout. The chosen cloudy silhouette and cream/lavender palette are this project's art direction, not a claim that those sources prescribe a particular look.

`Scripts/World/Planet/Authoring` contains `PlanetAuthoring`, `PlanetPlacement` and `PlanetProjection`. Editor tools and the disposable preview shader live in `Scripts/Editor/Planet/Authoring`. There is one authoritative `PlanetTileMap` on the inactive Runtime Planet, initially 72 × 36 cells, and 228 authored placements including the ship and spawn point.

X is longitude, Z is latitude and Y is radial elevation. At radius 75 the plane spans approximately 471.24 × 235.62 metres. Left/right edges join; each north/south edge collapses to a pole. A tangent rotation frame preserves heading and tilt. `PlanetAuthoring` executes at order -10000, applies all poses and active states, hides the authoring hierarchy, then activates spherical gameplay. The runtime target references remain internal to the prefab.

Projection runs once per scene load. There is no per-frame conversion, deformation or second physics world. Plane terrain, water and prop previews are editor-only, never serialized or included in a player build, and released before Play. Terrain painting updates only the ground preview on release. Model picking rejects bounding-box misses and lazily reuses one query collider per shared mesh. The inactive placement handles remain in memory during Play but have no update callbacks.

The old sphere placement windows, layout generator, test terrain, direct sphere painting and `PlanetSurfaceAlign` snapping code have been retired. `PlanetEnvironmentObject` preserves streaming grouping without the previous per-object `LateUpdate`. Spherical terrain generation, locomotion, combat distances, water, environment streaming and scene travel are still required for spherical gameplay. `ChapterOneHologram` is an independent prefab; it does not inherit the gameplay world.

This architecture reduces authoring duplication and removes unnecessary callbacks. It does not establish an FPS improvement on target hardware; profile a device build before making that claim.

## Verification

**BackHome > ChapterOne > Validate Stage Fog** checks GLES3/Vulkan/Metal shader compilation, shared cell ownership, locked-land coverage, rendering/resource budgets, deterministic generation without changing gameplay randomness, GPU before/after captures, ordered unlocks and all-open rebuild. A crimson-edge regression captures every A/D land boundary at gameplay and low camera angles (18 views in ChapterOne), checks for exposed red terrain while excluding yellow sand, and compares against a fog-free control. Movement regressions cover seven approaches including diagonals, long steps and the longitude seam, different body sizes, repeated small steps, outward escape, changed fog width, disable/re-enable, unlock cleanup and managed allocations. It uses a disposable world with saving disabled, and checks production prefab/scene files and player progress remain unchanged. Results and PNGs: `Logs/StageFog/`.

**Validate Stage Fog In Play** runs an isolated, unsaved progression through the live production prefab. It checks the animated boss unlock, movement blocking during the reveal and release afterwards, mesh/material destruction, disable/re-enable, interruption during a reveal and unchanged player save, and captures a portrait view at the A/B boundary. It restores the original persistence/background settings after exiting Play. Report: `Logs/StageFog/play-results.txt`.

**BackHome > ChapterOne > Validate Stages** checks both stage hierarchies, placement bindings, biome and road ownership, sequential boss completion, duplicate victories, save/reload, movement across locked terrain, distance streaming and deferred creature spawning. It uses isolated prefab contents and a temporary save key; production assets and the player's progress remain unchanged. Report: `Logs/PlanetStage-validation.txt`.

**Validate Stage Play Sessions** runs two Play sessions in the open Galaxy scene. It verifies that the three Stage A bosses are registered, B content and clouds stay locked after the first two victories, the third opens only B, partial/completed progress survives component recreation within one Play, and the next Play starts at A again. The player is protected and automatic combat disabled during the check; persistent PlayerPrefs are verified unchanged. Report: `Logs/PlanetStageSession-validation.txt`.

**BackHome > ChapterOne > Validate World** runs 4,096 projection/rotation/tangent cases, authored pose/scale checks, terrain seam brushes, topology checks, 128 perspective/vertical ray hits on a transformed plane, exact prop picking, preview cleanup, palette add/project/remove, preset round trips and edited prefab save/reload. It uses temporary contents and verifies that production scene and prefab files remain unchanged. Report: `Logs/PlanetAuthoring-validation.txt`.

**Validate Play And Travel** opens the production scene, drives player movement, invokes death/restart, takes off to Ship and returns through the hologram. It checks single planet/player/HUD, camera, creatures, terrain render/collider agreement, 64 surface probes per world entry, radial alignment, stable terrain version and absence of editor preview physics. The Ship check verifies cockpit camera, pilot hands, hologram mesh, click collider and beam reference. Report: `Logs/PlanetPlayValidation.txt`. Save open scenes first. These are Editor regression checks, not a device benchmark or exhaustive gameplay QA.

## Authoring limits

This is an equirectangular chart: spacing and object footprints distort towards the poles. Seam-crossing props are not drawn twice in the editor. The flat preview uses a lower-resolution ground grid; spherical generation defines exact polar caps and gameplay collisions. Large props remain rigid. Changing terrain does not automatically move existing props; use **Snap To Ground** where needed.
